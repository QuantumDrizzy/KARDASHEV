## 0. THE METHOD

$$C_{program} = C_{dev} + C_{hardware}(P) + C_{launch}(M, \$/kg_{dial2}) + C_{ops}\cdot T$$

Three honest rules:

1. **Benchmarks are [MEASURED] from real industries**, including the counterexample (Western nuclear cost *inflation* — learning curves are earned, not granted).
2. **Learning curves are explicit**: cost declines 5–25 % per production doubling in most industries (solar ~20 %/doubling, Swanson's law `[MEASURED]`; batteries ~18 %; aircraft ~15 %) — **with Western nuclear as the measured counterexample** (negative learning in the West since the 1970s; Vogtle 3&4 ≈ $15/W installed `[MEASURED]` vs. China ~$3/W).
3. **The unpriced line is declared, not hidden**: the first closed self-replicating ecology has never been built — its dev cost has no precedent. Bounded below by ITER-class programs (~$25 B) and above by ISS-class cumulatives (~$150 B). **Flagged [MEASURE: impossible until attempted]** — the budget's honest hole.

## 3. THE VERDICTS (what the model says)

### 3.1 Era I is affordable at constant policy — the headline

Summing everything (both K1 paths, all industry, all assays): **Era I ≈ $1–3.5 T spread over ~60–100 years** — against world energy investment of **$3 T *per year, today***. **The K1 climb costs roughly 1–2 % of the world's existing energy-investment flow, sustained for a century.** No new economics required. The bottleneck is not money; it is the two dials and the gates — which is exactly what PHASE0/ACCELERATION said from the power side. The dollar model and the watt model now agree.

### 3.2 The first-unit wall (where the money actually bites)

Program costs are front-loaded: the *first* Rijke-10, the *first* hive, the *first* closed ecology carry flagship-class dev premiums ($10–150 B each) while delivering learning, not watts. The learning-curve arithmetic (80 % curve on 170 hives → fleet ≈ 5 × marginal-unit cost) means **the fleet is affordable iff the first unit is funded without demanding it produce** — the classic first-plant financing problem, now with a number on it.

### 3.3 The dial-2 coupling, in dollars

Every halving of $/kg halves the launch component of every program: mercury-forge's launch line ($10–25 B at $200–500/kg) becomes $2–5 B at the scoreboard target. **The dial-2 program is simultaneously the B-reduction program and the budget program** — three ledgers, one gate (the TAU-GATE convergence, extended).

### 3.4 The cost model independently reproduces ERAS — the proof of value

Run the numbers without the ERAS calendar and the sequencing falls out by itself: **venus-fleet priced launch-only comes to $2–20 T — not fundable before in-situ manufacture exists**; post-crossover, with ISRU mass, it drops two orders. The dependency calendar of ERAS is, on inspection, a **budget calendar**: each decade-gate is exactly the point where a program's price class drops an order of magnitude. Map and business plan agree — that is the model's validation.

### 3.5 Where dollar accounting ENDS (the scope boundary)

Above the crossover, the swarm grows by self-manufacture at ISRU cost: the marginal dollar cost of swarm watts → ~0, and the binding currency becomes **energy, matter, and time** (the Radiator Theorem and the closure ledger, not the treasury). **This model is an Era-I instrument.** At K2 the "budget" is a reinvestment fraction of the energy flow itself (the doubling law's energy-consistency check: at K2, 2 % reinvestment = 2×10²⁴ W of growth capacity — the τ = 3 yr law is energy-consistent with a 2 % reinvestment rate by three orders of margin `[DERIVED]`).

## 5. THE MODEL'S OWN GAPS (declared)

1. Dev costs of first-of-kind *space* industry: analogy-bounded only (§0 rule 3).
2. Ops costs over decades: amortization assumed in the class totals, not modeled.
3. The learning-curve constants: borrowed from terrestrial industries — space-industry learning may differ ×2 `[ASSUMED]`.
4. Currency/century: everything in 2026 USD; a 100-yr program's real resources are watts and matter — which is why §3.5 ends dollar accounting.
