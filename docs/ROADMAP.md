# Roadmap

One sitting. `docs/SYNTHESIS.md` is the argument and `docs/NUMBERS.md` is the catalogue. This is the order those measurements already imply for the real world.

It adds no module, no partner, no price and no date. Every figure below is already computed in `src/lib`. This file is not regenerated by `npm run findings`. If it disagrees with a module, the module wins.

A **hypothesis** is an input the code labelled and did not measure. A **budget** is a dial we picked. An **extrapolation** holds a measured rate past the years that rate was observed. None of those is a completed step.

## 1. Where the measurement says we are

Sagan 1973: `K = (log₁₀ P − 6) / 10`. IEA total energy supply for 2023 is 620 EJ/yr, which is **19.6 TW**, which is **K ≈ 0.729** (`kardashev.ts`). Displayed as 0.73.

Type I is 10¹⁶ W. The gap is **×509 in watts**. That is nine doublings. Type I is **5.8%** of the sunlight Earth's disk already intercepts (`facts.ts`).

`forecast.ts` is the only place a year is computed, and it computes two:

| figure | kind | to Type I |
|---|---|---|
| 1.8%/yr, IEA TES 2013–2023, held constant | **extrapolation.** The rate is measured. Nothing supports "forever". `yearsInertial()`, reproduced as `inertial-years-to-type-i` | **~349 yr** |
| λ = 0.62 | **hypothesis.** Kind `assumed`. Assumes collaboration. Each doubling lasts 0.62× the previous | **~101 yr** |

Quote the second only beside the first. Divided by the thirty industrial doublings in `isru.ts`, the same hypothesis is a claim that off-planet industry doubles every **3.4 years**. That claim is exactly as strong as λ, and no stronger.

`route.ts` will not hand back a year unless a scenario is passed in, and this page does not pass one. The scenario table in `SYNTHESIS.md` §2.23 stays there. It is not a schedule.

The gap that binds first is not ×509. Under the **+0.1 K budget we picked**, waste heat binds at **192 TW**, **K ≈ 0.828**, **×9.8** today's supply, **3.29 doublings** (`sequence.ts`, `thermal.ts`). **+0.5 K** is the same dial at five times the power. The greenhouse crossover at 1,530 TW is a comparison. The land wall at 5,960 TW is the physical rung. The inertial years on that ladder are the same extrapolation as the ~349 yr row, so they are not repeated as dates.

## 3. What does not move the gap

**A cost curve** (`learning.ts`). Photovoltaic modules learn at **22%** per doubling in **$/W**. The binding gap is **×505 in kg/m²**. An illustrative **6%** in mass — explicitly not a measurement — buys about **×6** over the build's thirty doublings. `grid-cost.ts` is a dated capex sketch and is kept out of the physics tests for that reason. Wright's axis is units built, in the units of the gap. A cheaper watt does not lighten a square metre, and it does not move either year in §1.

**Hiding the signature, or finding it** (`signature.ts`). Bolometric contrast against the Sun is **2.6×10⁻¹¹**. Mid-infrared contrast at 9.06 µm is **2.5×10⁻⁸**, one part in 41 million, after a spectral advantage of **×943**. The SETI null result constrains Type II, ten orders above this object. Occultation at **32.6 ppm** is the prediction the chain makes; a detection would be evidence about area, and it would add no watt. The mass lever wants the silicon hot, and every detection axis gets worse as T rises. Concealment is not a step on this path.

**Beaming the power down** (`beam.ts`). Orbital generation with the load left on Earth dissipates **1.20 W** of crust heat per useful watt. The land advantage against ground PV is **×3.8**. That is a real argument about area. It is not an argument about K.

**Compute efficiency** (`substitution.ts`, `industry.ts`). A factor of **×52** puts Type I *computation* under the 192 TW ceiling, against a logic runway of **×1,232**. Spending that runway needs an economy that is **≥95.8%** computation. Today compute is **0.27%** of supply, and the mix buys **×2.31**, which is the industrial runway. The ×52 gate is one the thesis needs shut. Industry, transport and materials have no Landauer term that closes ×509.

**Quantum, the grid, the operator.** `qsim.ts` measured a **29-qubit** wall and an energy at which one brute-force gate costs a second of Type I near **80 qubits**. Real hardware avoids that bill. Avoided cost is not generated power, and `pqc.ts` is the same fact on the channel. `grid.ts`: every intercontinental tie is HVDC, a planetary synchronous grid is excluded, and a wire with infinite capacity still has nothing to send when every cluster is short. `operator.ts`: **1,315 ms**, which misses the 0.5 s arrest window. That latency binds a control loop. It does not sit on the numerator of K.

**A faster mind** (`acceleration.ts`). The first wall is **3.29 doublings** in every regime the module runs, because σT⁴ does not negotiate. The organisational gap between λ and the `e/p` floor is **×159**. Closing it is a statement about logistics, tooling and allocation. `qualification.ts` puts a floor under the part of that statement which is physical: a 69-year life is not known in less than 69 years.

## 4. The next measurement

One item. Lunar dust, on the rover chain in `surface.ts`.

The module already ranks it as the most likely thing that makes the rover result harder. The line it replaces is the wheel energy

    E/m = μ · g · d

with `ROLLING_RESISTANCE = 0.1`, marked assumed, and with no dust term in it. Over a pole-to-equator great circle that is **4.44×10⁵ J/kg**. The **22,914** rovers and the **0.44%** of embodied energy are downstream of that line. Rover mass (**10 t**) and speed (**10 km/h**) are assumed too, and the fleet is linear in both.

Three other absences stay open, and stay unmeasured. They are not this step and they are not done:

- rolling resistance **0.1**, swept and not defended; soft regolith and slopes can be worse
- terrain: no grade, no craters, no route a vehicle can follow
- the thermal cycle of crossing between **40 K** shadow and **400 K** sunlight

**Done** is one of two filings:

1. A number in `surface.ts` whose input is a **cited** lunar abrasion rate or a **cited** lunar dust flux, locked by a test. The Grün (1985) flux in `environment.ts` is the interplanetary meteoroid flux at 1 AU, partially checked against Love & Brownlee. It is the wrong object. It is not a surface wear rate and it is not to be reused as one.
2. An explicit refusal, of the kind `surface.test.ts` already locks: `dustWear` is absent. If no citable abrasion or dust flux can be named, the module does not grow a wear field.

Cited, and not worn. Hollick and O'Brien (2013), *Space Weather* 11, 651-660, doi:10.1002/2013SW000978, opened at the authors' repository record of the paper. The abstract prints an upper limit of order **100 μg cm-2 yr-1** of net accretion, conditional on the cell damage being entirely dust and calibrated on MLS-1. That figure is in `surface.ts`. It is not a wear rate. `dustWear` stays absent. Rolling resistance 0.1 stays assumed. The journal PDF itself returned 403.
