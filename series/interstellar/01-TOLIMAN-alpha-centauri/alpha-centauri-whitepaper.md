### 1.2 The binary is the architecture constraint (and the clock)

Every structure around A or B lives inside a three-body problem with a 79.9-year heartbeat:
- Orbits must stay below a_crit ≈ 2.8 AU (Holman–Wiegert scaling at μ ≈ 0.45, e = 0.52; modern integrations agree: < ~3 AU) — **both HZs fit**, but anything beyond ~3 AU is on borrowed stability.
- S-type orbits pump eccentricity cycles of ~7,200 yr `[EST, numerical studies]` — any manufactured world inherits a slow climate oscillator by celestial mechanics alone. Design input, not a surprise.
- **Periastron/apastron illumination:** binary separation swings 11 → 36 AU; cross-illumination and the tidal geometry of large habitats vary over the 80-yr cycle. The system has *seasons* at two clocks: 79.9 yr (binary) and the 7,200-yr eccentricity cycle.

### 1.3 Proxima's slow orbit

Proxima sits ~13,000 AU from A/B `[EST]`, orbiting every ~10⁵·⁵ yr — and appears to be **near its current closest approach** `[EST]`. Implication: the geological arrangement we design against today is a snapshot; over 10⁵ yr the outer system's perturbation geometry drifts. Nothing in the program horizon cares; the ledger notes it for the K3-era bookkeeping.

## 2. THE TWO-TRACK PROGRAM (manufacture vs. occupy)

### 2.1 Track 1 — MANUFACTURA: real estate at A/B (the power-plant play)

The Type III ladder's portfolio economics said it first: **G/K stars are the power plants; M-dwarfs are the savings accounts.** A and B are the only plants in range. With no planets to negotiate with, the program is:

1. **Inventory the raw material** `[MEASURE]`: no debris disk confirmed at A/B; in-system mining depends on unconfirmed minor-body populations. The honest boot pulls raw mass from *outside*: the seed ship's feedstock, or Proxima-system material hauled inward at ~13,000 AU cost `[EST: prohibitively expensive — flag]`. The inventory question is a P0 imaging campaign (Wagner-class contrast limits currently exclude planets above ~Neptune at HZ; much smaller bodies are wide open).
2. **Build in the HZ, inside the stability wall:** orbital habitats (rotating, the O'Neill lineage) at 1.2 AU (A) and 0.75 AU (B) — 1 g spin-gravity, K-star-grade insulation on the B side, G-star-grade albedo control on the A side. Power: A delivers ~1,200 W/m² at 1.23 AU, B ~890 W/m² at 0.71 AU — solar is *strong* here (compare Titan's 15 W/m², KRAKEN's electric farms).
3. **The 80-year season doctrine:** periastron (11 AU) doubles cross-illumination and tidal stress vs apastron (36 AU) — habitat attitude and thermal control budgets are scheduled on the binary phase, not the year.
4. **Scale to local-K2:** each star's swarm is a Type-II budget around 1.5 L☉ / 0.5 L☉ — the INVICTUS playbook transplants verbatim (the Radiator Theorem doesn't care which star).

### 2.2 Track 2 — OCUPACIÓN: Proxima b (the furnished house, hardened)

1. **Characterize before commit (OKEANOS transplant):** no transits → no transmission spectroscopy. Available characterization: thermal phase curves, direct imaging at 0.05 AU separation (hard `[MEASURE]`), reflected-light spectroscopy margins. **The honest position: b's atmosphere is unknown and may stay unknown until a spacecraft is there.** The program budgets a characterization orbiter *before* any surface plan.
2. **Design for the floor:** an airless, tidally-locked, flare-bombarded rock is the baseline. Anything more (atmosphere, ocean, eyeball geometry) is upside, not assumption.
3. **The bunker-first habitat:** storm shelters are not a room (DEIMOS) but a *design law*: XUV/superflare hardening = TANTALUS mini-magnetosphere doctrine scaled up (active magnetic shielding at habitat scale `[VERIFY: shielding mass/field sizing for M-dwarf flares — Mars-shielding literature exists, flare-era sizing doesn't]`), plus burial under native regolith (the mole doctrine, again — it worked on Mars, Titan, Mercury...).
4. **The locked-planet geometry:** permanent dayside/nightside. If an atmosphere exists, heat transport to the nightside is the climate question; if not, the terminator ring is the only temperate real estate — a 100-km-wide city strip by geometry.

### 2.3 The portfolio verdict (why both tracks, why this order)

Track 1 and Track 2 are the two halves of the Type III ladder's star-class economics instantiated at one address: **A/B pays the bills, b is the long-term account.** The honest sequencing: characterization orbiters first (cheap, both tracks), manufacturing pilot second (needs raw-mass inventory — the P0 gap), Proxima surface last (needs the atmosphere answer and the flare-hardening fleet).

## 4. PROXIMA b: WHAT WE KNOW, WHAT WE DON'T

Known: 1.07 M⊕ minimum mass (RV — true mass up with inclination), P = 11.19 d, a = 0.0485 AU, 0.65 S⊕; d = 0.26 M⊕ at 5.12 d (hot sub-Earth, locked); 0.65 S⊕ places it in the consensus HZ.

Not known (the honest column):
- **True mass, radius, density** (no transit → no radius).
- **Atmosphere: nothing detected, and no transit means transmission spectroscopy is off the table.** Thermal phase curves and secondary-eclipse constraints are weak at this separation `[MEASURE]`.
- **Water, magnetic field, rotation state** (locked is "likely", not measured).
- **Proxima c: disputed** — later RV analyses argue against the ~5.2-yr candidate. Treat as nonexistent until measured.

The eyeball hypothesis (dayside ocean under a frozen nightside, if atmosphere + water exist) is a *rendering*, not a measurement. The program treats it as fiction until the characterization orbiter reports.
