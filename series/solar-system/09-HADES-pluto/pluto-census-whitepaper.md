## 2. THE DEMOTION AND THE DEFINITION (the census question)

The honest scientific framing the schoolbook version obscures:

1. **Two taxonomies, both correct.** *Dynamical*: a planet is what gravitationally dominates its orbit (8 bodies — a useful orbital-mechanics category). *Geophysical*: a planet is a hydrostatic-equilibrium world (rounded by its own gravity — ~130+ bodies, a useful *world*-category). The IAU 2006 definition adopted the first and *named* the second "dwarf" — the fight was about database labels, not physics.
2. **The clause fails off-Earth.** "Clearing the neighborhood" is undefined for exoplanets, binary systems, and free-floating planets — the definition that removed Pluto cannot classify most known planets. `[KNOWN_LIMIT: the definition war is live; this volume takes the census, not the side]`
3. **The census verdict:** the system has **8 dynamical planets, ~130+ geophysical planets, ~400 satellites, and 10⁵+ KBOs** — the map the 20th century drew was a *special case*. The schoolbook of Antonio's childhood (16 tomos, Pluto among the planets) was not wrong; it was using the other taxonomy.
