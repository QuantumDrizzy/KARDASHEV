## 2. THE TWO ANOMALIES (the physics the flyby left unanswered)

### 2.1 The heat ledger — one class, opposite budgets

| | Absorbed solar (W/m², global mean) | Internal flux | Emitted/absorbed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Jupiter | 12.2 | 5.4 | 1.67 |
| Saturn | 3.7 | 2.0 | 1.78 |
| **Uranus** | **0.9** | **~0.04** | **1.06** |
| **Neptune** | 0.38 | 0.43 | **2.61** |

Same formation class, same distance band, *seven times* the normalized internal output. This is planetary science's largest unexplained spread.

**Leading hypothesis (§7's first sim target): compositional layering.** If the interior is stratified by composition (water-rich over hydrogen-rich, with diffusive interfaces), convection is *suppressed* — heat flows by conduction/diffusion across stable layers, and the deep reservoir's 4.5-Gyr reservoir never reaches the surface. Uranus = layered (sealed); Neptune = somehow un-layered. The mega-impact connection: a 1–3 M⊕ oblique impact (required for the 97.77° tilt in collision models `[HYPOTHESIS]`) would have *stirred* the interior — the counterfactual question is why Uranus is the *sealed* one. Either the impact history inverted the expectation, or layering formed later (e.g., the CH₄ raining out into stratified layers `[VERIFY: methane-water miscibility at interior P-T]`). Both worlds' dynamos (offset, tilted, multipole-ish) are consistent with convection confined to thin shells — the dynamo geometry is *evidence* for the layering picture `[HYPOTHESIS, the sim exists to test it]`.

### 2.2 The tilt (and the season trap)

- 97.77°: each pole gets 42 years of continuous day, then 42 of night; seasons are 21 Earth-years. Voyager hit Uranus at *southern midsummer* — the bland, featureless planet in the images may be a **seasonal artifact**: one photograph of one face at one season `[KNOWN_LIMIT: all Uranus morphology is a single-season snapshot]`. IR observations since (Keck/JWST-era) show storm activity and polar vortices appearing decades after the flyby.
- Program consequence: any orbiter mission *must* plan multi-year science against a 21-year seasonal clock — the UOP-class mission is designed to span the equinox, not the solstice.

### 2.3 The wind ledger (Neptune's excess)

Supersonic jets to 600 m/s (2,100 km/h) under 1.5 W/m² of sunlight: per unit solar input, Neptune's circulation is ~10× more energetic than Jupiter's `[EST from wind-energy densities]`. The energy source is internal (2.61× absorbed) delivered through the same possibly-layered interior — the wind puzzle and the heat puzzle are one problem: how a *sealed* interior drives the system's fastest winds. Current GCMs reproduce neither fully `[KNOWN_LIMIT: ice-giant GCMs are the least mature in the series — Saturn-class models adapted; the §7 module is the field's frontier]`.

### 2.4 The dynamos (geometry as evidence)

Both ice giants have magnetic dipoles displaced ~0.55 R from center and tilted ~47–59° — multipole-heavy, non-axisymmetric. No other planets in the series look like this. Interpretation `[HYPOTHESIS]`: the dynamo lives in a *thin convecting shell* (probably the ionic-water layer), with the deep interior stably stratified — the field geometry is the layering hypothesis made visible. Two flybys of magnetometer data; zero in-situ field maps. The UOP-class magnetometer survey is the anomaly's cheapest test.

## 3. THE INTERIORS: A NATURAL EXOTIC-MATERIALS LABORATORY

### 3.1 Superionic ice — the universe's default water

At ~50–100+ GPa and 2,000–5,000 K, water enters the **superionic** phase (ice XVIII): an oxygen crystal lattice with hydrogen atoms flowing freely through it — simultaneously solid and liquid, electrically conductive (which is *why* the dynamos live there). Lab-confirmed in 2019 by ramp-compression `[Millot et al., NIF]`; the phase diagram between 50 GPa and multi-TPa is still being mapped.

- **The series' honest天文 note:** most of the universe's water — inside ice giants and the ubiquitous water-rich sub-Neptune exoplanets — exists in this phase, not in Earth's oceans. OKEANOS's "Earth's sister ocean" correction has a cosmic sequel: *Earth's ocean is the rare phase.*
- The ice giants are the only places in the system where superionic water exists at natural planetary scale — in-situ sounding (gravity + magnetic + Doppler seismology-ish) calibrates the phase diagram for every exoplanet interior model ever run.

### 3.2 The diamond rain (E2's honest settlement)

Methane dissociation at ~150 GPa → carbon crystallization → diamond precipitation through the mantle `[lab: Kraus et al. 2017]`. Physically real, economically unreachable (§0). The laboratory dividend: the same experiments established shock-compression carbon chemistry — the *technique* transfers to swarm-era materials science; the *deposit* does not. Filed under: places the series refuses to lie about.

### 3.3 The "ices" that aren't

The term "ice giant" is a formation-history label, not a temperature label: the water/ammonia/methane "ices" exist as hot, dense, *fluid* superionic/ionic layers at thousands of K. The class name survives because of how they *formed* (beyond the snow lines, solids of volatiles), not how they *are*.

## 4. THE GALACTIC CALIBRATION (the reference class, E7)

### 4.1 The exoplanet statistics

Kepler/TESS-era census: planets of 1.8–4 R⊕ — **sub-Neptunes, the ice-giant analog class** — are the most common planet radius type around Sun-like stars (~30–50 % occurrence). Jupiter-analogs: ~1–2 %. Earth-analogs: the headline, but the *mode* of the galaxy's planet distribution is ice-giant-class. Consequence: **the most common planet in the Milky Way is a type we have visited exactly once, with instruments designed before its existence was known to be typical.**

### 4.2 What calibration actually means here

Every transit/radius/atmosphere-retrieval model for sub-Neptunes (the K2-18-class worlds, the mini-Neptune atmospheres, the water-world hypotheses) parameterizes against Uranus/Neptune data. The chain: exoplanet radius → interior model → *calibrated on two worlds whose interiors rest on one gravity flyby each*. The error bars in the exoplanet literature's most common class inherit our n = 1 ignorance. An orbiter + probe at either ice giant is, in calibration value, **an in-situ mission to 10¹⁰ planets**.

### 4.3 The probe payload (the UOP-class core, extended by the series)

- Entry probe: in-situ atmospheric composition (D/H, He/H₂, CH₄, noble gases — the formation fingerprint), T/P profiles to 10 bar, cloud chemistry — the *only* in-situ data for the class.
- Orbiter: gravity field (interior structure — the layering test), magnetometer survey (the dynamo/layering evidence), Doppler/imaging over ≥1 season, radiometry (the heat budget's *spatial* structure — Voyager measured a number, not a map).

## 5. TRITON: THE KUIPER ARCHIVE AT THE FRONT DOOR

### 5.1 The captured sample

Triton is a KBO that the Neptune system caught (retrograde, 157° inclination; leading mechanism: **binary-exchange capture** — Triton arrived as the lesser member of a Kuiper binary and got kept `[Agnor & Hamilton 2006 lineage]`). The trans-Neptunian region's primordial chemistry — 4.5 Gyr at 38 K — was *delivered* to the system's front door without a 40-AU Kuiper mission.

- Surface: 38 K (coldest measured), N₂ ice with CH₄/CO/CO₂, **cantaloupe terrain** (diapiric cryovolcanic morphology — a world that *did* have heat), N₂ geysers (Voyager imaged 8-km plumes — solar-driven N₂ sublimation jets in a 14-µbar atmosphere).
- Interior: NH₃-water ocean `[HYPOTHESIS: probable, from density + radiogenic + tidal history]` — OKEANOS-class habitability question, one door further out.
- The doom clock: tidal decay → Roche destruction in **~3.6 Gyr** → a ring. Borrowed inventory, like Phobos (vol. 6) — on a geologic clock instead of a programmatic one.
